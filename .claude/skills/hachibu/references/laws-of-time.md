# Laws of time and work

Read this when a plan decision rests on one of these laws, or when the user asks about one. Name the law in one line when it explains a choice. Most "laws" here are observations or rules of thumb, not scientific laws; say so if the user asks.

## Contents
- The core five: Parkinson, Pareto, Carlson, Hofstadter, triviality
- Effort and avoidance: Laborit, Illich, Yerkes–Dodson
- Decisions and systems: Hick, Kidlin, Gall, Goodhart, Murphy
- Tools: Eisenhower matrix, Zeigarnik effect, two-minute rule
- How they combine in one plan

## The core five

**Parkinson's law.** "Work expands so as to fill the time available for its completion" (C. Northcote Parkinson, *The Economist*, 1955). *Coach use:* every block gets `start_time` + `due_time`. Admin gets 15–30 min. If a task has no end, give it one. Parkinson's second law ("expenditure rises to meet income") is used in `hachibu-money`.

**Pareto principle (80/20).** Vilfredo Pareto observed ~80% of Italy's land owned by ~20% of people; Joseph Juran generalised it as "the vital few and the trivial many". The ratio is illustrative, not exact. *Coach use:* each day and week, name the vital few (max 3) that move current strategies, and place them first.

**Carlson's law.** Work done in one continuous stretch takes less time and is better than the same work split into interrupted pieces. From Swedish economist Sune Carlson's 1951 study of executives, who were interrupted roughly every 20 minutes. *Coach use:* one task per deep block, 60–90 min, no list-switching, notifications off. Batch similar work (all filming in one block).

**Hofstadter's law.** "It always takes longer than you expect, even when you take into account Hofstadter's Law" (Douglas Hofstadter, *Gödel, Escher, Bach*, 1979). Related to the planning fallacy (Kahneman and Tversky). *Coach use:* +30% to estimates, +50% for new kinds of work. The 20% hara hachi bu margin absorbs the rest.

**Law of triviality (bikeshedding).** Parkinson (1957): a committee approves a nuclear plant quickly but argues at length about the bike shed, because everyone understands the shed. *Coach use:* when time goes to a logo colour, caption or setting while the hard task waits, name it and timebox the choice to 10 minutes, then pick the user's first instinct or the simpler option.

## Effort and avoidance

**Laborit's law.** Named after biologist Henri Laborit: we tend to avoid what's unpleasant and drift toward what's rewarding right now. *Coach use:* the hard Peak task goes first in the Peak window, before email or social media ("eat the frog", via Brian Tracy). Admin frogs go to the sweep, not the Peak window.

**Illich's law.** Attributed to Ivan Illich: past a certain point, more continuous work yields less, then negative, output. *Coach use:* break after ~90 min; no Peak work late at night.

**Yerkes–Dodson law.** Performance rises with arousal up to a point, then falls (1908, originally on mice; simplified in popular use). *Coach use:* some deadline pressure helps; a rising overdue count means lower the load, not add motivation.

## Decisions and systems

**Hick's law.** Decision time grows with the number of options (Hick and Hyman, 1952). *Coach use:* give at most 3 options, with one recommended.

**Kidlin's law.** "If you write the problem down clearly, the matter is half solved." A popular maxim with unclear origin. *Coach use:* when the user is stuck, ask for the problem in one sentence and put it in the task description.

**Gall's law.** "A complex system that works is invariably found to have evolved from a simple system that worked" (John Gall, *Systemantics*, 1975). *Coach use:* start with the smallest routine that works; add only after it holds for two weeks.

**Goodhart's law.** "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure" (Marilyn Strathern's phrasing of Charles Goodhart, 1975). *Coach use:* don't chase ticked boxes. In reviews, check whether done work moved a goal.

**Murphy's law.** "Anything that can go wrong will go wrong." *Coach use:* a Ma buffer before launches, payments, races and travel. Plan B for the one thing that must happen.

## Tools

**Eisenhower matrix.** Urgent/important 2×2, popularised by Stephen Covey from an Eisenhower quote. *Coach use:* in overdue triage, important-not-urgent work is protected first; urgent-not-important is batched or delegated; neither is a drop candidate.

**Zeigarnik effect.** Unfinished tasks stay on the mind (Bluma Zeigarnik, 1927; replication is mixed). *Coach use:* park open loops as BudgeBit tasks; end each deep block by writing the next step into the task description so the mind can let go.

**Two-minute rule** (David Allen, *Getting Things Done*). *Coach use:* tasks under 2 minutes go in the admin sweep, not on the calendar.

## How they combine in one plan

1. Pareto picks the vital few.
2. Laborit puts the hardest first in the Peak window.
3. Carlson makes each a single unbroken block.
4. Parkinson gives it an end time.
5. Hofstadter pads the estimate.
6. Hara hachi bu stops the day at ~80%.
7. Triviality and Illich are the alarms during the day.
8. Kaizen closes with one small change.
