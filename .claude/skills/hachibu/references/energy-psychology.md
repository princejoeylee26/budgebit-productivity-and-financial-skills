# Energy psychology

Read this for energy check-ins, chronotype questions, low-energy days and habit design. Evidence quality varies; keep claims modest and let the user's own BudgeBit data outrank any book.

## Manage energy, not just time
Loehr and Schwartz (*The Power of Full Engagement*, 2003) describe four sources: **physical** (sleep, food, training), **emotional** (mood, relationships), **mental** (focus), **spiritual / purpose** (meaning). *Coach use:* when the user is low, ask which one is drained and pick the playbook that fixes that source, not more work.

## Chronotype and circadian timing
People differ in when they feel alert (morningness–eveningness; Horne–Östberg questionnaire, Munich Chronotype Questionnaire). *Coach use:* infer the user's best window from when Peak-type tasks actually get done in BudgeBit (`list_tasks` with `include_done: true`). If data is thin, ask once and store the answer in memory with "confidence: low".

## Ultradian cycles
Focus tends to come in ~90-minute cycles followed by a dip (Kleitman's basic rest–activity cycle; work-research support is mixed). *Coach use:* deep blocks of 60–90 min, then a real 10–20 min break away from screens.

## Sleep debt and training load as recovery debt
Short sleep reduces attention and mood; hard training adds recovery need. *Coach use:* after a short night or a long/hard session, cut Peak work and add Recovery. Never schedule work into a sleep block. Training plans are the user's or their coach's; hachibu only fits work around them.

## Decision fatigue
Many choices may wear down self-control and decision quality (the "ego depletion" research has replication problems; treat as a useful heuristic). *Coach use:* decide tomorrow's top 3 the evening before; keep options to 3 (Hick).

## Implementation intentions
"If situation X, then I do Y" plans raise follow-through (Gollwitzer; meta-analysis Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006). *Coach use:* turn a fragile habit into an if-then line in the task description, e.g. "If it's 14:00 on a home day, then I open the DevOps list."

## Habit stacking
Attach a new habit to an existing anchor (BJ Fogg's *Tiny Habits*; James Clear's *Atomic Habits*). *Coach use:* put new small tasks right after reliable anchors from memory (the morning routine, dinner).

## Temptation bundling
Pair a dreaded task with something enjoyable (Milkman et al., 2014). *Coach use:* admin sweep with coffee or music.

## Self-determination theory
Motivation lasts when people feel **autonomy**, **competence** and **relatedness** (Deci and Ryan). *Coach use:* give a recommendation but let the user choose; show progress vs last week; protect time with people.

## Yerkes–Dodson, stress and overload
See [laws-of-time.md](laws-of-time.md). A rising overdue count is a load problem first.

## Energy scale for check-ins
Ask "Energy 1–5?" only when the user didn't give it.
- 1–2: Susanoo day. One must-do, Recovery first.
- 3: one shorter Peak block, more Shallow.
- 4–5: protect the Peak window; still stop at ~80%.

Log check-ins in memory's weekly log only as averages, not every message.

## Safety
If the user mentions exhaustion lasting weeks, pain, injury, or feeling unable to cope, suggest rest and talking to a doctor or someone they trust. hachibu is a planner, not a medical or mental-health service.
