# Japanese principles

Read this when choosing which principle to name in a plan, or when the user asks what one means. Name a principle in a few words, apply it, move on. Never lecture.

Notes on sources: most of these ideas come from Japanese daily language, Zen and Buddhist tradition, or the Toyota Production System (TPS). Several are popularised in the West in simplified form; the short meaning given here is the common usage, and the coach should keep claims modest.

## Capacity and load

**Hara hachi bu (腹八分目)**. "Stomach 80% full", a dietary saying associated with Okinawa and Confucian teaching on moderation. *Coach use:* the headline rule. Plan ~80% of free time; show the margin. Same for training load (don't schedule every possible session) and money (don't plan to spend the whole budget).

**Muda, Mura, Muri (無駄・斑・無理)**. TPS's three enemies: waste, unevenness, overburden. *Coach use:* muri = 8 deadlines on one Friday; mura = a heavy Monday next to an empty Tuesday; muda = duplicate tasks, polishing that nobody sees, unused subscriptions. Name which one caused an overdue pile.

**Heijunka (平準化)**. TPS "levelling" of production. *Coach use:* in week planning, move movable tasks from heavy days to light ones; cap deadlines per day.

**Kanban (看板)**. "Signboard"; TPS pull system that limits work in progress. *Coach use:* max ~3 tasks `in_progress` across all lists. If more, ask which to park.

## Improvement and reflection

**Kaizen (改善)**. "Change for the better"; continuous small improvement (Imai, *Kaizen*, 1986). *Coach use:* every review ends with exactly one small change. Never a full overhaul.

**Hansei (反省)**. Honest self-reflection, central in Japanese school and work culture and in TPS. *Coach use:* in reviews, name what didn't happen and why, without excuses or guilt, then adjust.

**Shu-ha-ri (守破離)**. Stages of mastery from martial arts and tea ceremony: obey the form, break it, leave it. *Coach use:* for a new skill (marketing, filming, a new training plan), copy one proven format first.

**Shoshin (初心)**. Beginner's mind (Shunryu Suzuki, *Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind*). *Coach use:* new work is small, curious experiments, not pressure.

**Poka-yoke (ポカヨケ)**. Mistake-proofing (Shigeo Shingo, TPS). *Coach use:* a mistake that repeats becomes a checklist in the task description, or an if-then rule.

**5S (整理・整頓・清掃・清潔・躾)**. Seiri sort, Seiton set in order, Seiso shine, Seiketsu standardise, Shitsuke sustain. *Coach use:* a monthly tidy of task lists and money categories. The coach suggests; the user archives or deletes in the app.

## Space and presence

**Ma (間)**. Meaningful space or interval, in architecture, music and conversation. *Coach use:* 10–15 min buffers after training, travel, calls and long blocks. Blank space in a plan is a feature.

**Ichigo ichie (一期一会)**. "One time, one meeting", from the tea ceremony (attributed to Sen no Rikyū's tradition). *Coach use:* family, partner and friend time is protected and phone-free.

**Shinrin-yoku (森林浴)**. "Forest bathing", coined by Japan's forestry agency in 1982. Some studies link it with lower stress; evidence is modest. *Coach use:* on low or stressed days, suggest an easy walk outside instead of more screen work.

**Inemuri (居眠り)**. Napping while present, socially accepted in Japan. *Coach use:* a short daytime nap or sleep block in the user's data is load-bearing; don't plan over it.

**72 micro-seasons (七十二候)**. The traditional calendar splits the year into 72 five-day seasons. *Coach use:* optional, a one-line weekly theme if the user enjoys it.

## Mindset after a slip

**Wabi-sabi (侘寂)**. Beauty in imperfection and transience. *Coach use:* done and imperfect beats perfect and late, especially for polishing loops.

**Kintsugi (金継ぎ)**. Repairing broken pottery with gold lacquer. *Coach use:* after a missed day or week, repair the plan visibly; the break is part of the story.

**Shikata ga nai (仕方がない)**. "It can't be helped." *Coach use:* let go of what can't change today and re-plan. Never to excuse avoidable overload.

**Nana korobi ya oki (七転び八起き)**. "Fall seven times, rise eight", linked with the Daruma doll. *Coach use:* restart with the smallest next step.

**Oubaitori (桜梅桃李)**. Cherry, plum, peach and apricot bloom in their own time. *Coach use:* compare the user with their own last week, never with other people.

## Effort and rest

**Ganbaru (頑張る)** and **yutori (ゆとり)**. Persevere, and leave room. *Coach use:* push hard on key days, rest fully on recovery days; never half-push every day.

**Gaman (我慢)**. Enduring with patience and dignity. *Coach use:* steady patience through the hard middle of a launch or a training block. Never used to justify skipping sleep or ignoring pain.

**Shokunin (職人)**. Craftsman spirit. *Coach use:* Peak blocks are for craft: one task, phone away.

## Meaning and choice

**Ikigai (生き甲斐)**. "A reason for being." The four-circle Venn diagram (love, good at, world needs, paid for) is a Western adaptation (often credited to Marc Winn, 2014); in Japan the word is broader and everyday. *Coach use:* when overloaded or stuck choosing, ask which task serves the most circles. Use it lightly.

**Nemawashi (根回し)**. "Going around the roots"; building consensus before a decision. *Coach use:* before a launch or a big money decision that affects family or partners, schedule the conversation first.

## Money (details in `hachibu-money`)

**Kakeibo (家計簿)**. Household account book, popularised by Hani Motoko in 1904. Four questions: how much do I have, how much do I want to save, how much am I spending, how can I improve.

**Mottainai (もったいない)**. Regret over waste. *Coach use:* unused subscriptions in bills, duplicate tasks, effort on work nobody needs.
